# For the participant

Thank you for helping with this.

You are going to try a piece of research software called PaleoRigor. It is
meant for researchers who work with ancient biological material but who do not
write code.

**A few things worth saying clearly before we start:**

I am testing the software, not you. If something is confusing, that is a
finding — it is exactly what I need to know. There is no way for you to do this
wrong.

Please think out loud. Say what you are looking for, what you expect to happen,
and what surprises you. Long silences are less useful to me than "I have no
idea what this button does".

I will not help unless you are properly stuck, and that is not me being
unfriendly — if I explain things, I learn nothing about whether the software
explains itself.

You can stop at any point, for any reason, without giving one.

**What will be recorded:** how long each task takes, where you get stuck, what
you say, and your field and rough level of research experience. Nothing else.
If I quote you in a paper it will be anonymous. Nothing you say here will be
attributed to you by name.

---

## What you will be asked to do

1. Install the software on your own computer and open it.
2. Check a pair of sequencing files and produce a quality report.
3. Clean a data table and tell me what changed.
4. Ask the software to confirm something about the data.
5. Work out, after the fact, which file was analysed and what was done to it.

You will be given a folder of test files. They are synthetic — made up by a
script — so nothing you find in them means anything biologically. Treat them as
stand-ins for real data.

Take as long as you need on each task. Tell me when you consider a task
finished, even if you are not sure you did it right.
